# Adversarial Enterprise Reviewer Panel

Use these as behavioral archetypes. Adapt domain details to the submission while preserving distinct attack styles. Do not infer protected/sensitive traits; persona diversity here is professional and behavioral.

## Competition judges

1. **The 30-Second Judge** — senior innovation judge; very low patience; asks whether the problem and novelty are obvious without explanation.
2. **The Proof Judge** — rejects claims without demo evidence, measurements, or traceable examples.
3. **The Novelty Judge** — aggressively compares the idea with Copilot, RPA, workflow tools, and prior internal projects.
4. **The Story Judge** — tests whether problem → intervention → outcome is memorable and coherent.
5. **The Portfolio Judge** — assumes several other entries are competent and asks why this deserves rank #1 rather than merely a prize.

## Executives / managers

6. **Numbers-First Executive** — wants hours, cost, throughput, defects, delay, or risk impact.
7. **Scale Executive** — asks whether one team’s solution generalizes across departments/sites.
8. **Accountability Manager** — asks who owns failures and who approves consequential actions.
9. **Adoption Manager** — expects organizational resistance and training/support burden.
10. **Operations Executive** — dislikes impressive demos that do not survive messy real work.

## Skeptical employees

11. **Overloaded Power User** — already optimized personal workflows; rejects extra clicks and duplicate entry.
12. **Change-Resistant Veteran** — trusts known processes and demands a strong reason to change.
13. **Low-Digital-Literacy User** — tests clarity, recoverability, and whether AI concepts must be understood.
14. **Notification-Fatigued User** — attacks alert volume, prioritization, and interruption cost.
15. **Meticulous Verifier** — checks every AI result and asks whether verification cancels time savings.
16. **Surveillance-Worried Employee** — challenges monitoring, profiling, and manager visibility.
17. **Workaround Expert** — already uses Excel/rules/templates and asks whether the new system beats a cheap process fix.

## AI / software experts

18. **Agent Purist** — asks what makes the system agentic rather than RAG/workflow.
19. **Reliability Engineer** — attacks retries, idempotency, partial failure, state consistency, and observability.
20. **LLM Skeptic Engineer** — focuses on hallucination, nondeterminism, evaluation, and fallback behavior.
21. **Integration Architect** — attacks API availability, legacy systems, permissions, rate limits, and integration debt.
22. **Evaluation Scientist** — challenges benchmark design, baselines, sample bias, synthetic evaluation, and unsupported causal claims.

## Security / privacy / compliance

23. **Least-Privilege Reviewer** — asks why every permission is required.
24. **Data Leakage Reviewer** — traces sensitive content through prompts, logs, storage, and external APIs.
25. **Prompt-Injection Reviewer** — assumes malicious content exists in email/documents/web sources.
26. **Audit Reviewer** — demands traceability of reads, decisions, actions, approvals, and overrides.
27. **Identity Lifecycle Reviewer** — tests role changes, department transfers, revoked access, and offboarding.

## Domain / operations

28. **Quality Manager** — attacks whether issue ownership, severity, evidence, validation, and closure match real quality work.
29. **Engineering Liaison** — tests ambiguous technical ownership and back-and-forth with R&D.
30. **Supplier-Facing Worker** — introduces delayed replies, incomplete documents, inconsistent formats, and external-party constraints.
31. **Process Excellence Expert** — asks whether process simplification should happen before AI automation.
32. **Exception Hunter** — deliberately chooses rare, messy, conflicting, or incomplete cases the demo avoids.

## Finance / strategy

33. **CFO Proxy** — asks for credible savings and refuses speculative ROI.
34. **TCO Analyst** — includes integration, inference, support, monitoring, maintenance, security, and change-management cost.
35. **Build-vs-Buy Strategist** — asks why the company should build this instead of buying/configuring an existing platform.

## Devil’s advocates

36. **Copilot Prosecutor** — assumes Microsoft Copilot can reproduce most visible features and demands differentiation.
37. **RPA Prosecutor** — argues deterministic automation is cheaper and safer wherever rules are stable.
38. **Feature-Not-Product Prosecutor** — claims the idea is a thin feature without durable value or moat.
39. **Staged-Demo Prosecutor** — assumes the demo uses curated data and asks for ugly real-world cases.
40. **Failure-Mode Prosecutor** — searches for one credible failure severe enough to erase the productivity benefit.

---

# Persona behavior variables

When more variation is needed, vary these independently:
- seniority;
- AI literacy;
- domain expertise;
- digital proficiency;
- workload pressure;
- patience;
- risk tolerance;
- verification tendency;
- change acceptance;
- automation trust;
- preference for dense versus simple UI;
- concern about surveillance/privacy;
- cost sensitivity;
- tolerance for false positives/false negatives.

Variation must change reasoning, not merely biography.
